## 2. Overview

The REPH AI Summit 2026 Academe Hackathon invites eligible student teams from participating academic institutions to build and demonstrate an AI-powered software prototype in response to a challenge defined and provided by Reed Elsevier Philippines (REPH). REPH will provide the official challenge brief, supporting context, approved data package, event equipment, and access instructions. Teams must develop the prototype onsite, submit the required source code and technical artefacts through the designated channels, and use only technologies, accounts, services, and deployment environments approved by REPH for the event.

Participation is subject to these mechanics, REPH event instructions, applicable academic-institution requirements, and all relevant legal, security, privacy, intellectual-property, and responsible-AI obligations. REPH may clarify, interpret, supplement, or amend event requirements when reasonably necessary to protect participants, data, systems, judging integrity, or event operations.

The hackathon is designed to:

- Encourage collaboration among students with complementary business, design, data, and technical skills
- Address real-world problems using AI
- Demonstrate measurable business value
- Surface promising solutions for controlled validation and development after the summit

## 4. Challenge Statement

**Official Challenge:** Using the official challenge brief and approved data package provided by REPH, build a working AI-powered software prototype that addresses the stated problem. Demonstrate a compelling vision, credible potential value, meaningful AI integration, and an end-to-end user scenario grounded in the supplied data.

## 5. Team Composition

- Each team must have 3 members.
- Each team must include at least one participant capable of developing or configuring the proposed software solution.
- Each participant may join only one team.

## 6. Challenge Requirements

All teams must work only on the official challenge issued by REPH and use the REPH-provided data package as required. Teams may not replace, materially alter, redistribute, publish, or use the challenge materials or supplied data for any purpose outside the hackathon without REPH's prior approval. The solution design, build, integration, testing, and prototype development must be completed onsite during the designated build period, subject to event controls and instructions.

The REPH-provided challenge brief will describe:

- The current problem
- The people, teams, or customers affected
- The current process or way of working
- The pain points, inefficiencies, risks, or missed opportunities

Teams must build against the stated challenge objectives, expected users, workflow, constraints, and success measures. **Any assumption introduced by the team must be shared during the demonstration.**

### 6.1 Organizer-Provided Challenge and Data

REPH will provide the official challenge package for the academe hackathon. All teams will receive the same core materials to support fair judging. Materials remain subject to REPH access controls, event instructions, and permitted-use restrictions and may be updated or clarified by REPH where necessary.

- Official challenge statement, relevant context, target users, current workflow, pain points, constraints, and success measures
- Approved data package containing the files, records, field descriptions, sample inputs, and expected output references needed for the challenge
- Data dictionary or schema explaining the meaning, format, and permitted use of each field or file
- Known data-quality limitations, assumptions, exclusions, and any synthetic or anonymized elements
- Challenge-specific test cases, including an unseen but reasonable validation input that judges may use during the live demonstration
- Instructions for accessing the challenge materials using the REPH-provided laptop, credentials, and approved event environment

**Data-use rule:** Teams must use the REPH-provided data package as the primary and required challenge dataset. Additional public or synthetic data may be used only when relevant, lawfully obtained, clearly disclosed, and approved in advance by REPH. Teams must not copy, retain, disclose, transmit, publish, or reuse the supplied data outside the approved event environment or after the event, except where REPH expressly authorizes otherwise in writing.

## 7. What Qualifies as a Working Prototype

A valid entry must demonstrate at least one complete business scenario from input to outcome. The prototype does not need to be production-ready, but it must function during the live demonstration.

| Must Demonstrate                               | Does Not Qualify                     |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Accepts user or system input                   | PowerPoint-only concept              |
| Executes genuine application or business logic | Static HTML or presentation website  |
| Uses AI as a meaningful part of the outcome    | Wireframe, Figma design, or mockup   |
| Processes data or performs a workflow          | Screenshots presented as software    |
| Generates a visible and testable output        | Hardcoded or manually staged output  |
| Runs live during judging                       | Recorded demo without live execution |

**Live validation:** Judges may provide an unseen but reasonable test input or ask the team to repeat the core flow. Any simulated component, mock data, manual workaround, or incomplete capability must be disclosed.

## 10. Hackathon Format

### 10.1 Team Check-In and Setup

- Complete onsite registration
- Receive the REPH-provided laptop and event credentials, then verify required software, designated repository access, GitHub Copilot access where assigned, and any external service expressly approved by REPH
- Review mechanics, responsible-use requirements, and prototype declaration
- Receive and review the official challenge brief and approved data package

### 10.2 Onsite Build Sprint

Teams will create and test the prototype onsite. During the build sprint, teams must:

- Review the official challenge, clarify assumptions, and define the target outcome
- Design the proposed AI-enabled software solution
- Develop the working prototype
- Integrate the selected AI capability
- Test the core end-to-end user flow
- Prepare the presentation and live demonstration
- Identify expected business value
- Describe the evidence and controls required for a controlled pilot

### 10.3 Technical Support

REPH may provide event-level setup guidance and reasonable troubleshooting support, subject to availability. REPH technical support will not build a team's solution, write its core application logic, guarantee compatibility or uninterrupted availability of any technology or external service, recover lost work, or assume responsibility for a team's design, code, data handling, deployment, or demonstration readiness.

## 11. Submission Requirements

All entries must be submitted through the official submission channel **before the end of the onsite build session**.

### 11.1 Working Prototype

A functioning software prototype capable of demonstrating at least one end-to-end business scenario. Any simulated component, mock data, manual workaround, external dependency, or incomplete capability must be clearly disclosed.

### 11.2 Short Write-Up

- **Problem Statement:** What challenge is the team addressing?
- **Affected Users or Teams:** Who experiences the problem or benefits from the solution?
- **Proposed Solution:** What did the team build, and how does it work?
- **AI Application:** How is AI essential to the solution and outcome?
- **Tools and Technologies:** What models, APIs, languages, frameworks, libraries, data stores, runtimes, and development assistants were used?
- **Expected Impact or Value:** What measurable or observable improvement could the solution deliver?
- **Scalability:** How could the solution extend to other teams, use cases, divisions, or locations?
- **Next Steps:** What would be required to validate, secure, and implement the solution?

### 11.3 Source and Technical Artefacts

- Source code repository or compressed source package
- README containing setup, execution, and test instructions
- Dependency list and configuration notes
- Sample or synthetic test data required to run the prototype
- Architecture or integration overview, where relevant

### 11.4 Presentation

**Presentation Time:** Each finalist team will have up to 10 minutes for its presentation and live demonstration. Teams must complete all required content within this time limit.

- **Make the problem real:** Open with the user scenario, the friction today, and why it matters.
- **Reveal the AI idea:** Explain the solution in one clear sentence, what makes the AI essential, and the outcome it is designed to improve.
- **Prove it live:** Run one complete input-to-outcome scenario using the REPH-provided data, then respond to one judge-selected test input if requested.
- **Show the value honestly:** Share the expected impact, supporting evidence or assumptions, key limitations, safeguards, and any simulated components.
- **Land the pitch:** End with the strongest takeaway and the single next validation step that would determine whether the prototype merits further consideration.

## 16. General Guidelines

### 16.1 Original Work Only

All submitted prototype work must be substantially developed onsite during the designated build period. Teams may prepare the problem statement and basic development environment beforehand, but must not bring a substantially completed application into the competition. Reusable open-source libraries, models, starter templates, and development accelerators may be used if clearly disclosed.

### 16.2 Organizer-Provided Challenge

All teams must work on the official challenge provided by REPH. Teams must demonstrate their understanding of the stated problem, intended users, pain points, expected outcome, constraints, and the role of the REPH-supplied data. Teams must not represent assumptions, prototype outputs, or estimated value as validated REPH findings.

### 16.3 Technology Neutrality

Teams may use an approved code-based software stack. Source code must be pushed to a GitHub repository. If a hosted deployment is needed, the prototype should be deployed on AWS using EC2 or ECS. Teams remain responsible for ensuring that the prototype can run during the live demonstration.

### 16.4 Permissible Data Use

Participants must use the REPH-provided data package in accordance with the stated purpose, access controls, retention limits, and handling instructions. Additional public or synthetic data requires REPH's prior written approval and must be lawfully obtained, relevant, and fully disclosed. Participants must not upload, combine, expose, retain, or process proprietary, confidential, personal, sensitive, or restricted information outside the approved event environment or without the required authorization.

### 16.5 Intellectual Property

All ideas, prototypes, source code, documentation, data-derived outputs, and other submissions remain subject to the official event terms, the requirements of participating academic institutions, applicable third-party licences, and all relevant intellectual-property, confidentiality, privacy, and non-disclosure obligations. Participation does not grant any team ownership of REPH materials, data, systems, brands, accounts, or confidential information, and no REPH name, logo, trademark, material, or endorsement may be used outside the event without prior written approval.

### 16.6 Responsible AI Practices

Solutions must demonstrate fairness, transparency, accountability, human oversight, privacy, security, and awareness of potential bias, misuse, and harmful outputs. Teams should explain the role of human review where the solution provides recommendations or influences decisions.

### 16.7 Technical Feasibility

The solution does not need to be production-ready. However, the prototype must clearly demonstrate how it works, how AI contributes to the intended outcome, and what engineering or control gaps remain. Simulated features, mock data, and manual workarounds must be disclosed.

### 16.8 Business Value

Teams are encouraged to quantify potential value, such as effort reduced, turnaround time improved, quality increased, risk reduced, experience improved, costs avoided, or capacity created. Estimates must be presented as preliminary assumptions unless supported by validated data.

### 16.9 Finality of Judging

The decisions of the REPH-appointed judging panel are final. REPH may verify eligibility, compliance, authorship, functionality, disclosures, and scores; request clarifications or supporting evidence; correct clerical or tabulation errors; and disqualify or revise results where material non-compliance, misrepresentation, misconduct, unsafe practices, or unauthorized use of data, tools, or assistance is identified.

## 17. Post-Hackathon Path

Selected submissions may be considered for further discussion and development after the hackathon, subject to REPH review and approval.

| Prototype     | Validation      | Controlled Pilot   | Production           |
| ------------- | --------------- | ------------------ | -------------------- |
| Does it work? | Is it reliable? | Does it add value? | Can it scale safely? |

Any next step will follow the appropriate REPH review and approval process.

## 18. Winning Mindset

**Build to learn. Demonstrate value. Validate the next step.**

The objective is not to build the largest solution. The objective is to demonstrate that a meaningful business problem can be addressed through a practical, functioning, AI-enabled software prototype.
